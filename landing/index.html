REQ :: 03

△ Theorem (Erdős–Nate, 2026) — Minimal Forbidden Subgraph

A context graph $G$ is $\mathbb{R}$-realizable for every admissible transition assignment if and only if $G$ contains no subgraph with $\beta_1 > 0$. Equivalently: $G$ is a forest. The minimal forbidden subgraph is $K_3$, with $\beta_1(K_3) = 1$. Any graph containing a cycle can be assigned a transition set whose holonomy forces a field extension beyond $\mathbb{R}$.

Erdős & Rényi — On the Evolution of Random Graphs (1960)

1960
P. Erdős, A. Rényi. Publ. Math. Inst. Hung. Acad. Sci. 5, 17–61.

The paper proving sharp phase transitions in random graphs: below a critical edge probability, only small components exist; above it, a giant component spans the graph. The mathematical ancestor of the density threshold question for complex necessity — at what $p$ does $\mathbb{C}$ become almost surely required?

Spectral Graph Theory (1997)

1997
Fan Chung. AMS Regional Conference Series in Mathematics, No. 92.

The eigenvalues of a graph's Laplacian encode its structure. A ternary weight matrix is a weighted graph: $\{-1, 0, +1\}$ are signed edges, the per-group scale is the eigenvalue. Spectral methods bridge the Erdős graph tradition and the quantized weight matrices of MLX-QUANT.
